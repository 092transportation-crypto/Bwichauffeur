# Airport Transfer Tips & Tricks

## The Details That Actually Matter

After a decade of running airport transfers across BWI, DCA, IAD, and PHL, certain patterns show up constantly — small habits that separate a completely smooth trip from an avoidable scramble. None of these are complicated. Most take thirty extra seconds at booking time. Here's what actually makes a difference.

## Give the Flight Number, Not Just a Time

The single most valuable piece of information for any airport pickup is your **airline and flight number** — not your estimated landing time. A flight number lets your chauffeur's system track the actual aircraft, including gate changes, delays, and early arrivals, and adjust the pickup automatically. A guessed clock time doesn't account for any of that. If you book through a form that only asks for a time, call and add the flight number directly — it's the difference between a chauffeur who already knows you landed 25 minutes late and one who's been waiting at the curb wondering where you are.

## Build Real Buffer Into Departure Timing

For BWI departures, TSA lines run longest on Friday and Sunday evenings and during holiday travel weeks. Build in **2 hours before domestic departure** and **3 hours before international departure** as your buffer for pickup timing, working backward from your boarding time, not your flight time. This isn't overly cautious — it's the realistic floor for the airport's busiest windows, and a pickup timed to a "just enough" window is the single most common reason travelers miss flights that were entirely avoidable.

## Know Where You're Actually Being Picked Up

At BWI, licensed limo and black car pickup happens on the **lower Arrivals level**, at a curb separate from the taxi stand and rideshare lot. If you're unsure exactly where to meet your chauffeur, ask at booking — a good dispatcher will describe the specific curb location or offer a meet-and-greet upgrade where the chauffeur comes to you inside baggage claim instead. Wandering the wrong level looking for your car wastes exactly the time a pre-arranged pickup was supposed to save.

## International Arrivals Need Extra Wait-Time Awareness

Clearing Customs and Border Protection after an international flight can take anywhere from 20 minutes to well over an hour, depending on how many international flights land in the same window. A properly booked international pickup should include **90 minutes of complimentary wait time**, not the standard 60 — confirm this specifically when booking an international arrival, since it's easy for that detail to get lost if you're booking well in advance of the actual trip.

## Pack Your Layover Buffer, Not Just Your Flight Buffer

If your trip involves a connection, the tightest realistic layover for a domestic-to-domestic connection at most hub airports is 45–60 minutes; international connections need considerably more. When you're booking ground transportation on the other end of a connecting itinerary, build your pickup timing around the connecting flight's actual scheduled arrival — and give that connecting flight's number, not the first leg's, for accurate tracking.

## Curb Etiquette That Speeds Everything Up

A few small habits keep pickup fast for you and everyone else at a busy curb: have your phone ready to text your chauffeur if you don't see them within a minute or two, keep loose items consolidated rather than spread across a luggage cart, and if you're being picked up with a group, designate one person to coordinate with the chauffeur while everyone else loads bags. None of this is mandatory, but it shaves real minutes off a pickup during peak arrival banks when a dozen cars are working the same curb simultaneously.

## What to Do If Your Flight Changes Mid-Trip

Gate changes, delays, and even airport diversions happen. With flight-number tracking, most of this is absorbed automatically — but if your flight is diverted to a different airport entirely (rare, but it happens during major weather events), call your dispatch number directly rather than assuming the system will catch it. A human conversation resolves an edge case faster than any automated tracking system.

## Traveling With Kids or Elderly Family

If your pickup involves young children needing car seats, or elderly family members who may need extra time or assistance at the curb, mention this at booking — not on arrival. Car seats need to be requested in advance so the right one is in the vehicle, and a chauffeur briefed on mobility needs will plan for a slower, more patient curb transfer rather than rushing a pickup that needs more care.

## The Return Trip Deserves the Same Attention

Travelers plan the arrival pickup carefully and then book the departure transfer as an afterthought. Apply the same rules in reverse: confirm your departure pickup time against realistic TSA buffers, not your flight time; confirm the pickup address if you're coming from somewhere other than home (a hotel, a client office, a wedding venue); and if your schedule shifts in the days before departure, update the booking rather than assuming the original time still works.

## When Something Actually Goes Wrong

Even with good planning, something occasionally goes sideways — a chauffeur runs late because of an accident on I-295, a gate assignment changes at the last minute. The fix in every case is the same: call the dispatch number directly rather than waiting and hoping. A live phone call with a real dispatcher resolves almost any airport transfer problem in minutes; a support ticket or app message does not.

## Packing Your Carry-On With the Ride in Mind

A small habit that makes curb pickup faster: keep whatever you'll need immediately (phone charger, a jacket, travel documents) in an easily accessible outer pocket rather than buried at the bottom of a packed bag. You'll spend less time digging through luggage at the curb while your chauffeur is trying to load the trunk, and you'll be settled and ready to go within seconds of getting in rather than still rearranging bags on the seat next to you.

## Time Zone and Red-Eye Considerations

Travelers arriving on a red-eye or a flight that crosses time zones sometimes miscalculate their actual local arrival time, especially on connecting itineraries. Double-check that the arrival time you're working from is in local time at your destination airport, not the time zone you departed from — a common and entirely avoidable source of confusion when booking a pickup weeks in advance for a trip you haven't thought through in detail yet. Flight-number tracking solves this automatically once the booking is made, but it's worth getting right at booking time so your own expectations match what's actually going to happen.

## Weather-Related Adjustments

Maryland weather — winter ice on I-295, summer thunderstorms that ripple through the entire Eastern Seaboard's flight schedule — regularly affects both driving conditions and flight timing. A licensed operator adjusts routes and pickup timing around weather automatically as part of normal operations, but if you know your travel date coincides with a forecasted weather event, it doesn't hurt to call ahead and confirm the plan rather than assuming everything will proceed exactly as booked. A quick proactive call is always easier than a reactive one made from a chaotic curb.

## Confirming Your Return Trip While You're Still Away

A simple habit worth adopting: confirm your return/departure pickup while you're still on your trip, not the morning you fly home. A short text or call a day or two before your return flight — verifying pickup time, address, and flight number — catches any mismatch (a changed flight, a different hotel than originally planned) while there's still time to fix it calmly, rather than discovering a problem the morning of departure with a flight to catch.

## First-Time Flyers and Nervous Travelers

If you're picking up a first-time flyer, an elderly parent unfamiliar with a large airport, or anyone anxious about navigating BWI alone, mention this at booking. A chauffeur briefed in advance can wait closer to the security checkpoint on departure, walk a nervous traveler through exactly where to go, or simply build extra patience into the curb interaction rather than treating it as a standard, rushed pickup. This kind of accommodation costs nothing extra and makes a genuine difference for the traveler it's meant for.

## Keeping a Simple Travel Folder

For travelers who fly frequently, a small habit pays off repeatedly: keep flight confirmations, chauffeur booking confirmations, and hotel details in one place — a folder, a notes app, a single email thread — rather than scattered across multiple apps and inboxes. When a chauffeur asks for a flight number at the curb, or when plans shift and you need to quickly text an updated pickup time, having everything in one accessible place turns a potentially stressful moment into a thirty-second lookup.

## The Short Version

Give your flight number, not a guessed time. Build real buffers for TSA and Customs. Know your pickup curb in advance. Flag kids, mobility needs, or extra luggage at booking. And when something changes, call — don't wait.
