# Wedding Limo Service in Maryland: A Couple's Planning Guide

## Why Wedding Transportation Deserves Its Own Planning Line Item

Most couples plan their venue, caterer, and photographer in exhaustive detail and then treat transportation as an afterthought booked in the final month. That's backwards — transportation is the one vendor that touches nearly every hour of the wedding day, from the getting-ready suite to the ceremony to photos to the reception to the getaway car, and a transportation mistake is the hardest one to fix on the actual day. This guide walks through the planning process the way we'd walk a couple through it on the phone.

## Step One: Map the Actual Day, Not Just the Ceremony

Before calling a single vendor, sketch your day hour by hour: where the wedding party gets ready, what time the ceremony starts, where photos happen, how far the reception venue is, and whether guests need shuttling from a hotel block. Maryland weddings frequently span two or three venues — a church or garden ceremony in one town, photos at a scenic second location like Quiet Waters Park or the Naval Academy grounds, and a reception at a barn venue, waterfront club, or downtown hotel. Every leg of that route needs a vehicle, a driver, and a buffer for Maryland traffic, which means transportation planning has to happen before you can finalize your day-of timeline, not after.

## Step Two: Choose Your Vehicle Mix

Wedding transportation rarely means one car. A typical Maryland wedding uses some combination of:

- **A luxury sedan (Mercedes S-Class)** for the couple's getaway car or the bride's arrival
- **An SUV (Cadillac Escalade or Suburban)** for the wedding party or immediate family
- **A Sprinter van (up to 14 passengers)** for the full bridal party or as a guest shuttle between a hotel block and the venue

Guest shuttles deserve special attention if your venue has limited parking or you want to keep guests from driving after an open bar — a single Sprinter running a loop between hotel and venue every 30–45 minutes solves both problems at once. ## Step Three: Book on the Right Timeline

Maryland's wedding season runs heaviest April through June and September through November, and the licensed vehicles suited to weddings — S-Class sedans, Sprinters — are the same vehicles corporate and airport clients want on any given Saturday. Book **60–90 days out** for a standard Saturday wedding during peak season; for a date during a particularly busy weekend (a holiday weekend, or a Saturday that overlaps with Naval Academy Commissioning Week in Annapolis), book even earlier. Off-peak winter weddings have more flexibility, but the core rule holds: the date is fixed the moment you sign your venue contract, so transportation should be locked in within the same month, not the same week as the wedding.

## Step Four: Build the Real Timeline With Buffers

A wedding-day driver is not just executing a route — they're absorbing every delay the day produces: hair and makeup running long, a photographer wanting five more minutes, a ceremony that starts fifteen minutes late because of DC-area traffic on the way in. Build 15–20 minutes of buffer into every leg of the transportation schedule, and communicate the full itinerary — including photo locations and reception load-in time — to your transportation coordinator, not just the pickup and drop-off addresses. A vendor who asks for this level of detail during booking is signaling they've done this before; one who only asks for a start time and address is not.

## Questions to Ask Before You Book

- Is the company **licensed and commercially insured**, and can they confirm that in writing?
- Will the **same chauffeur** stay with the wedding party all day, or does the vehicle change hands between legs?
- What's the **overtime policy** if the reception runs long and the getaway car is needed later than planned?
- Is there a **backup vehicle** in case of mechanical issue — and how quickly can it be deployed?
- What's included in the quoted price — is decoration (ribbons, "Just Married" signage) available, and is gratuity built in or separate?

## Coordinating With Your Other Vendors

Your transportation coordinator should talk directly to your day-of coordinator or planner, not just to you. Photographers need to know exactly when the car arrives for departure shots; venues need to know when the guest shuttle will be running its loop so valet or parking staff can plan around it. If you have a day-of coordinator, share the transportation vendor's dispatch contact with them directly during final-week vendor confirmations — it removes you from being the relay point on your own wedding day.

## Out-of-Town Guests and Airport Transfers

If a meaningful share of your guest list is flying in — common for Naval Academy families, destination-adjacent Eastern Shore weddings, or couples with out-of-state family — consider bundling airport transfers into your transportation plan. A block of pre-arranged BWI pickups for out-of-town family removes one more thing from their plate and yours, and a single vendor coordinating both the wedding-day fleet and guest airport transfers means one point of contact instead of a dozen individual Uber confirmations you're fielding by text the week of the wedding.

## What It Costs

Wedding transportation pricing depends on vehicle mix, hours booked, and mileage, but expect a luxury sedan for a half-day booking to start in the low-to-mid hundreds, with Sprinter vans priced by the hour for guest shuttle duty. Get a specific quote once your vehicle mix and timeline are set — a reputable vendor gives you a clear, itemized number rather than a vague range, and locks that price at booking rather than adjusting it as your date approaches.

## Rain Plans and Weather Contingencies

Maryland weather is unpredictable enough that any outdoor ceremony or photo plan needs a transportation contingency built in. If your ceremony has a rain backup location, make sure your transportation vendor has both addresses on file and a clear understanding of which one triggers on the day. The same applies to winter weddings — ask specifically how a vendor handles snow or ice on the day of, since a wedding date can't be rescheduled the way a business trip can, and you want a vendor whose fleet and chauffeurs are equipped for Maryland winter driving rather than one who might cancel on you.

## The Getaway Car Moment

The send-off is often the most photographed transportation moment of the entire day, and it deserves the same planning attention as the ceremony pickup. Confirm exactly where the getaway car stages, how guests will be directed toward it for a clean photo moment (sparklers, bubbles, or a simple clear path), and where it's actually driving to afterward — the reception hotel, the airport for an immediate honeymoon departure, or simply home. Some couples book the getaway car to continue directly to BWI for a same-night or next-morning honeymoon flight; if that's your plan, treat it as a second trip with its own timeline rather than an extension of the wedding-day booking, and give the chauffeur the flight number in advance.

## Budgeting Realistically

Wedding transportation is frequently one of the smaller line items in a Maryland wedding budget relative to venue, catering, and photography, but it's also one of the easiest to underestimate if you wait until late in planning and end up paying rush pricing for whatever's left. Get a specific, itemized quote once your vehicle mix and hours are set, and ask whether gratuity, decoration, and overtime are included or separate so your final number doesn't have surprises. Compare that quote against your overall budget early enough that you can adjust vehicle mix — dropping a guest shuttle, for instance — without scrambling in the final weeks before the wedding.

## A Vendor Who Has Actually Done This Before

The clearest signal of a transportation vendor worth trusting with a wedding day is specificity: they ask about your ceremony and reception venues by name, they know the typical drive time between popular Maryland wedding venues, and they ask about photo locations without being prompted. A vendor who only asks for a start time and an address hasn't necessarily done many weddings — and your wedding day isn't the moment to find that out the hard way.

## Decor, Signage, and Personal Touches

Many couples want their transportation to reflect the wedding itself — ribbons on the getaway car, a "Just Married" sign, or simply a clean, freshly detailed vehicle that photographs well. Ask what decoration options are available and whether the vendor supplies them or expects you to bring your own; either is fine, but it's better to know which one applies before the week of the wedding rather than discovering a gap in the final days. Some couples also request a specific vehicle color or model for the getaway car to match wedding photography plans — if that matters to you, request it explicitly rather than assuming any luxury sedan is interchangeable for photo purposes.

## The Rehearsal Dinner and Welcome Events

Wedding weekends increasingly involve more than just the ceremony day — a rehearsal dinner, a welcome party, a Sunday brunch. If your weekend includes multiple events across different venues, consider whether transportation coordination should extend beyond just the wedding day itself, particularly for out-of-town guests navigating an unfamiliar area for the first time. A single vendor coordinating the full weekend, rather than the wedding day in isolation, often simplifies planning meaningfully and gives out-of-town family one consistent point of contact for the entire visit.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How far in advance should we book wedding transportation in Maryland?

Book 60–90 days ahead for a peak-season Saturday wedding (April–June and September–November). Popular weekends, like those overlapping Naval Academy Commissioning Week, can fill even earlier, so book as soon as your date and venue are confirmed.

### How many vehicles does a typical Maryland wedding need?

Most weddings use a mix: a luxury sedan for the couple, an SUV for the immediate wedding party, and a Sprinter van either for the full bridal party or as a shuttle looping guests between a hotel block and the venue.

### What should we ask a wedding transportation vendor before booking?

Confirm licensing and commercial insurance, whether the same chauffeur stays for the full day, the overtime policy if the reception runs long, backup vehicle availability, and exactly what is included in the quoted price.

### Can transportation also cover out-of-town wedding guests flying in?

Yes. Many couples bundle a block of pre-arranged airport transfers for out-of-town family alongside their wedding-day fleet, giving guests one reliable pickup instead of individually coordinating rideshare.
